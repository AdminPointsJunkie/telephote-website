Telephote support

To use your iPhone as a webcam you need Telephote on iPhone and the free Windows companion on a Windows 11 x64 PC. Download the Windows companion, extract the whole ZIP and open Telephote.exe.

Connect with USB

Unlock the iPhone and connect a USB data cable. Choose USB on both devices. Tap Start camera on the phone and enter the six-digit code on Windows. If the PC cannot detect the iPhone, try a different data cable or use Wi-Fi. USB needs a compatible Apple USB driver on the PC.

Pair over Wi-Fi

Connect your iPhone and PC to the same local network. In Telephote on Windows choose Wi-Fi and Show QR code. On your iPhone choose Wi-Fi and Scan PC code. Allow Camera and Local Network access when prompted. If pairing fails, show a fresh code and check that the network allows devices to reach each other.

Use the camera in a call

Enable “Use in Teams and other apps” on Windows. In your meeting app’s camera settings choose the Telephote output, which Windows may list as “AvStream Media Device”. Select your meeting microphone separately. For the optional phone microphone preview, see microphone setup and availability. Tap Stop camera on iPhone to end the session.

Microphone preview

Desktop 0.5.0 requires iPhone companion 1.0 build 7 for microphone streaming. Earlier companions still support video. Enable microphone access on the phone and in the desktop Audio controls. Recordings are separate WAV files. A built-in plug-and-play Windows meeting microphone remains in development; it is not included in this download.

Linux preview

For Ubuntu 24.04+ and Linux Mint 22+ on Intel/AMD 64-bit PCs, use the Linux preview and setup guide. The archive includes the runtime. Meeting-app output needs an existing accessible v4l2loopback device, and iPhone USB needs an existing usbmuxd service. Live phone and meeting-app testing is still in progress.

Contact support

Send a public support request. Include your iPhone model, iOS/Android version, Windows or Linux version, connection type and what happened. Keep pairing codes, email addresses and personal images out of public reports.
